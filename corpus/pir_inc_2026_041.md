# Post-Incident Review: INC-2026-041

Review held 2026-03-05. Chair: Rachel Ocampo. Present: Sofia Brandt, Kenji Watanabe,
Alan Ferris, Tomas Berger.

## What happened

A macro-enabled spreadsheet was opened on a Finance workstation on 2026-02-16 and browser
credentials were harvested. The case was triaged Medium and closed on 2026-02-27.

## What went wrong

**Finding PIR41-01.** The initial Medium rating was reasonable on the evidence available.
The failure was not the rating; it was that no one asked what the harvested credentials
could reach. Nobody joined the workstation to its owner, the owner to their directory
groups, or those groups to the systems the Q1 access review had already documented. That
join is the single most valuable question after any credential theft and no runbook step
requires it today.

**Finding PIR41-02.** The stolen credentials were used on 2026-03-05 in INC-2026-045. Had
the severity been re-evaluated when the access-review data was published on 2026-02-20, the
account would have been reset with a forced re-enrolment rather than a self-service password
change.

## Decisions

1. The severity of INC-2026-041 is raised from Medium to Critical, effective 2026-03-05.
2. The SOC runbook gains a mandatory step: for every confirmed credential theft, record the
   affected host, its primary owner, the owner's directory groups and the systems those
   groups can reach, citing the current access review.
3. The re-rating does not reopen the ticket; INC-2026-041 remains closed.

## Not in scope

This review does not cover the remote access concentrator, the contractor offboarding
failure, or the exfiltration. Those are handled in the campaign review.

## Timeline of the review's own reasoning

The review began from the question of why a Medium-rated case in February became the origin
of a Critical case in March. Three candidate explanations were considered.

The first was that the triage was wrong on the evidence. The board rejected this. Kenji
Watanabe applied the runbook correctly and the runbook's criteria for Medium were met: a
single host, no execution beyond the initial process tree, no lateral movement, no
persistence.

The second was that the detection was too late. The board rejected this too. The EDR alert
fired within two minutes of execution and the ticket was open within eleven.

The third, which the board accepted, is that the runbook never asks what the compromised
identity can reach. That question is answerable from documents Vandelay already maintains, but
answering it requires joining four of them by hand, and nothing in the process requires anyone
to do that join.

## The join that was not made

To answer "what could the stolen credentials reach", an analyst must take the host named in
the alert, find its primary owner in the asset inventory, find that owner's directory groups
in the membership export, and find what those groups can reach in the privileged access
review. Four documents, three joins, no tool.

The board noted that the answer, once someone finally performed the join in late March, was
material enough that it would have changed the February response.

## Dissent

Kenji Watanabe recorded a dissent on the wording of the second action, arguing that forced
re-enrolment on every suspected credential theft would be disruptive enough that analysts
would avoid raising the suspicion. The board accepted the wording with "confirmed" rather
than "suspected" in response.
